02 / GIVE IT A TRY

Your first transfer.
Three simple steps.

Grab your phone and computer, and follow along.
↧

Choose the Windows installer on the official download page and follow the installation prompts.

Official download ↗All download links lead to official sources.
  1. 01

    Open both apps. Join one local network.

    Open LocalSend on both devices and connect them to the same local network where they can reach each other, such as your home Wi-Fi.

    ↳ An Ethernet connection works too. What matters is that the devices can communicate.
  2. 02

    Pick a file, then the receiving device.

    On the sending device, choose a file, photo, or text. Then select the recipient from the nearby devices list.

    ↳ Check the device name before you send.
  3. 03

    Accept on the other device. Done!

    Accept the transfer when prompted on the receiving device. After it finishes, check the destination folder. You can change the save location in Settings.

    ✓ Try sending one photo for your first transfer.

03 / NEED A HAND?

LocalSend FAQ
and troubleshooting

Start with the most common questions.

Official troubleshooting ↗
Does LocalSend need the internet?

Local file transfers do not require internet access, but both devices still need to be on a local network where they can reach each other. You still need a network connection, even without internet access. Downloading and installing the app generally requires internet access.

Why can’t I find the other device?

If LocalSend cannot find a device, check the following in order:

  1. Make sure LocalSend is open on both devices and that they are on the same reachable local network. Guest Wi-Fi may isolate devices from each other.
  2. If the receiving device runs Windows, check that your trusted home network is set to Private, and allow LocalSend to communicate on the local network.
  3. On an iPhone, iPad, or Mac, check whether LocalSend has Local Network permission in the system settings.
  4. If you use a VPN, check whether it allows local network (LAN) communication.

If the device still does not appear, note both operating systems, LocalSend versions, and your network setup. Then consult the official documentation or report an issue.

Source: LocalSend’s official troubleshooting guide.

Are files secure? Do they go to the cloud?

LocalSend transfers files between local devices without uploading them to the cloud first. It uses HTTPS encryption by default, and PIN verification is available. Check the sender and device name before accepting a transfer.

Read the official privacy policy ↗

How should I configure my firewall?

Follow the official instructions to allow LocalSend to communicate on your local network. The default inbound port is 53317 for both TCP and UDP. When your system prompts you, allow the app on a trusted network. See the official troubleshooting guide for details.

Is this the official LocalSend website?

This is an independent beginner’s guide by Open Trail, with no official affiliation with LocalSend. The app is developed by the LocalSend community and uses the Apache License 2.0. Every download link on this site points to an official source.
